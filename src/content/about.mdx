I'm an undergraduate in [Applied Computing in Finance at NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/business/education/undergraduate-programme/bachelor-of-applied-computing-in-finance), Singapore. I'm interested in fundamental equity research, especially small-cap biotech, and in building the software and hardware tools I use along the way.

## Background

I was born in a small city in China and spent my early childhood with my grandparents in the countryside, with a lot of freedom to explore. I moved to Singapore for school, studied at Hwa Chong Institution, and then served with the Singapore Police Force K-9 Unit as a narcotics detection dog handler and field training officer before starting university.

## My Investment Journey

I started investing during National Service, when a friend got me to sign up for a broker app through his referral link. I only meant to hold enough blue-chip stock to claim the bonus, but my timing was good: I caught the Nvidia rally and made my first thousand dollars, more than my monthly NS allowance at the time. Soon after, I rode Nebius through a sharp sell-off and its rebound.

Those quick profits pushed me further down the risk curve. I started reading Moomoo comment threads and Reddit forums and got drawn into penny stocks and meme tickers. Riding the MGOL short squeeze for a large gain warped my view of the market: I mistook luck for strategy. Reality caught up. My next penny-stock trades fell into the classic retail traps, holding the bag on pump-and-dumps, and I lost a substantial part of my capital.

That drawdown was the wake-up call. I stepped back from trading and focused on learning: investment wikis, financial analysis guides, SEC filings and long-form educational content. I stopped chasing tips and built a disciplined process: running custom stock screeners, reading clinical-trial pipelines, working through balance sheets, and doing full due diligence before putting any money at risk.

That process led me to my niche: **small- and micro-cap biotech**. These stocks are volatile, but they can offer asymmetric risk and reward to anyone willing to do the fundamental work that larger investors often skip, such as estimating trial success probabilities, building rNPV models and checking how long the cash will last. A series of disciplined, catalyst-driven positions in this space let me rebuild and grow the portfolio well beyond its earlier peak.
